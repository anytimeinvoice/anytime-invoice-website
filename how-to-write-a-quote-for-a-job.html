How to write a quote for a job

A checklist for tradies, builders and freelancers.

A clear quote wins the job and heads off arguments later. This is a practical checklist, not a legal template.

What to put on a quote

  • Your business name, ABN and contact details
  • The customer’s name and the site address
  • A quote number and the date you issued it
  • The scope: what the price covers, in plain words
  • An itemised price — labour, materials, and any call-out or travel
  • Whether the price includes GST, and the total the customer will pay
  • Your deposit and payment terms
  • How long the quote stays open — materials prices move, so pick a window you can honour
  • How the customer accepts

Say whether GST is included

If you are registered for GST, show the total price payable up front so there are no surprises. The ACCC says businesses should communicate the total price payable before the customer buys. Not sure whether you need to register? Read our guide to GST for sole traders.

Write down what is not included

Most disputes start with something the customer assumed was covered. List exclusions, and note that changes to the scope will be quoted separately.

A quote is not a tax invoice

A quote is your offer. Once the work is done, issue an invoice — and if you are registered for GST it needs to be a tax invoice. See what a tax invoice must show.
