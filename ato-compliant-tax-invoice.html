What makes a tax invoice ATO-compliant?

A short guide for Australian tradies, builders and freelancers who charge GST.

If you are registered for GST, your customer needs a tax invoice to claim a GST credit on what you sold them. If they ask for one, you must provide it within 28 days — unless the sale was $82.50 (including GST) or less.

What a tax invoice must show

For a taxable sale under $1,000, the ATO says the invoice must make these seven things clear:

  • That the document is intended to be a tax invoice
  • Your identity as the seller
  • Your Australian business number (ABN)
  • The date the invoice was issued
  • A brief description of what you sold, including the quantity (if applicable) and the price
  • The GST amount payable — shown separately, or as the statement “Total price includes GST” when the GST is exactly one-eleventh of the total
  • The extent to which each sale on the invoice is a taxable sale

For sales of $1,000 or more, the invoice also needs to show the buyer’s identity or ABN.

Common mistakes

  • Leaving off your ABN, or the words “tax invoice”
  • Showing a total with no indication of how much of it is GST
  • Mixing taxable and GST-free items without saying which is which
  • Issuing a “tax invoice” when you are not registered for GST — if you are not registered, you do not charge GST or issue tax invoices
