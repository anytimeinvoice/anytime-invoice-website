GST and invoicing for sole traders

When GST applies, when it does not, and how to keep your invoices straight.

Do you have to register for GST?

You must register for GST when your GST turnover is $75,000 or more, or when you expect it to reach that amount in the next 12 months. (For non-profit organisations the threshold is $150,000.) If you provide taxi or limousine travel, including ride-sourcing, you must register regardless of turnover. Below the threshold, registering is optional.

If you are registered

  • Charge GST on your taxable sales and issue tax invoices (see our guide to what a tax invoice must include)
  • Report and pay GST through your activity statement
  • Keep records of your sales and your business purchases

If you are not registered

You do not charge GST and you do not issue tax invoices. You can still send a clear invoice with your business details, a description of the work, the amount due and how to pay.

Keeping track without a spreadsheet

Anytime Invoice shows income, GST and overdue reports so you can see what is coming in, what is set aside for tax and who is running late. You can photograph a receipt and have it read and categorised automatically — 10 scans every 30 days on the free plan, unlimited on Pro. Customer statements give you a running balance per customer when they ask what they still owe.

The ATO generally expects businesses to keep tax-related records for five years, so it helps to have invoices and receipts in one searchable place.

Source: the ATO’s registering for GST guidance.
